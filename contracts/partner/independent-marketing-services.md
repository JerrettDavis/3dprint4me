This illustrative arrangement is between **{{company_legal_name}}**, operating as **{{company_dba}}** (the proposed client), and **{{partner_full_name}}** (the proposed service provider). It describes discrete promotional and referral projects, only if counsel determines that independent contracting is permissible under actual practices. ## [IMS-02] Offers, independence and no minimum
The client may present individual project offers stating services, geographic coverage, lawful methods, fees, conditions and deadlines. Provider may accept, decline or suggest revised terms without penalty and retains freedom to serve other clients and perform other odd jobs. Neither side guarantees a minimum volume of gigs, availability, hours or earnings. Day-to-day timing and method are generally at provider's discretion consistent with law and accepted terms. Attorney to test actual facts against applicable classification law; recital alone is not determinative.

## [IMS-03] Proposed services and authority limit
Permitted tasks may include introductions to consenting businesses, distribution of samples and literature only at lawful/authorized locations, factual reporting of interactions, and referrals given with permission. Provider is NOT empowered to set binding prices, make contractual commitments, collect or retain customer funds, promise manufacturing delivery or use company purchasing credentials.

## [IMS-04] Fees, budget and fair payment
Each individually accepted work order states the project fee and any separately defined qualified-lead or confirmed-sale bonus. Default new-work authorization budget is **${{weekly_authorization_default_usd}}** per program week, owner may authorize no more than **${{weekly_authorization_max_usd}}** without a program amendment, under a Monday-through-Sunday `America/Chicago` week. Budget is a limit on NEW commitments, not a limit on payment already owed. Accepted, properly earned compensation must be paid under applicable law even if client bookkeeping was mistaken. Fees, bonus attribution, payment timing **{{weekly_payment_day}}**, reasonable dispute and cure process and payment methods must be specified and reviewed by counsel.

## [IMS-05] Project acceptance and disputes
A project is accepted only through explicit, recorded assent to its frozen work order, with an audit of fee/terms. Submissions may be reviewed only against pre-disclosed objective criteria. A disputed result can be discussed and corrected, with records preserved. No arbitrary clawback or coercive forfeiture. Project cancellations and expenses to be defined by counsel, including work begun before cancellation.

## [IMS-06] Portal and personal autonomy
Client may provide a mobile web application to offer gigs, record business visits and consenting leads, issue receipts and allow optional start/stop activity entries. Such entries are voluntary and not mandatory shift or attendance checks. No real-time location monitoring. Provider can access own data and request correction or deletion as law allows. Client follows an approved privacy policy and does not require proprietary device purchases.

## [IMS-07] Promotional materials and appearance
Client supplies campaign-specific flyers, printed samples and business cards free of charge. Client may gift a branded shirt or other apparel; use is appreciated but **not required**. Provider is free to dress appropriately and use personal transportation or other lawful means. Expenses/reimbursement policy should be set within work order and reviewed for legal classification effects.

## [IMS-08] Transportation assistance separate
Any proposed **$200** bicycle assistance is outside the scope of this services arrangement if made as an unconditional personal gift, not an advance, wage or repayable amount; see separate draft acknowledgment. No project commitment or ongoing services obligation depends on the gift. If professional review recommends another treatment, document it as a separate, accurately characterized transaction.

## [IMS-09] Outreach legality, safety and respectful conduct
Observe applicable trespass/no-soliciting, privacy, permitted-distribution, traffic and bike safety restrictions; no mailboxes, harassment, coercion, unsolicited spam or misrepresentation. The provider may avoid unsafe work and contact client to reconcile deliverables. Client must furnish truthful marketing materials and not exploit provider's living circumstances or personal relationships in marketing.

## [IMS-10] Information, confidentiality and intellectual property
Use collected contact information only for campaign-specific consented purposes, prevent unauthorized disclosure, comply with the retention/deletion process, return or securely delete materials at end. Client owns company-provided design and brands; ownership of any original work product should be defined by counsel. No proposed noncompete or exclusivity restriction.

## [IMS-11] Taxes, insurance, classification and nonwaiver
Subject to legal determination of proper classification, contractor may have independent tax-reporting obligations, and client may have payment/reporting obligations. Nothing purports to waive statutory rights, shift nondelegable duties or conclusively establish contractor status. Counsel must evaluate Oklahoma and federal standards, insurance/indemnity, worker injury and bicycle risks. CPA must review W-9 and Form 1099 processes if classification supports this arrangement.

## [IMS-12] Ending projects and program
Either party may end participation without retaliation and no new gig offers are guaranteed after **{{program_end_date}}**. Obligations for completed or lawfully compensable authorized work, confidentiality and legally required recordkeeping survive.
